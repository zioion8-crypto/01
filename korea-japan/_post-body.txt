<h3 style="font-size: 1.2em; color: #0f172a; margin-top: 28px;">Why the ferry is the interesting one</h3>

<p>The New Camellia leaves Busan at 22:30 and docks in Hakata at 06:00, with disembarkation from 07:30 after Japanese immigration. Check-in opens at 16:30 and closes at 18:30; boarding closes at 20:00. Second class costs ₩150,000 plus ₩21,000 in fees from Busan, or ¥15,000 plus ¥4,900 from Hakata. Round-trip and student discounts ended on 30 September 2026, and bookings open six months ahead to the day.</p>

<p>Three things to know before you build a trip around it. It does not sail every day: in the October–December 2026 calendar there is no Busan departure on certain Saturdays and no Hakata departure on the Sundays after them. It cancels when the forecast wave height passes six metres. And a Busan departure booked on the Japanese site is converted at the operator’s own monthly rate, so price both directions in your own currency.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0; color: #854d0e; font-size: 0.95em;"><strong>💡 Rule that saves a day:</strong> put the crossing in the middle of the trip, never on the first or last day. A cancelled ferry on day 11 is an inconvenience; on the last day it is a missed flight.</p>

<h2 style="font-size: 1.5em; color: #0f172a; margin-top: 44px; border-bottom: 2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 10px;">3. The refund rule that changed on 1 November 2026</h2>

<p>This is the change most 2025 guides still get wrong. Japan moved from “pay the tax-exclusive price at the shop” to a <strong>refund method</strong>: you now pay the tax-inclusive price and claim the consumption tax back after a customs check at the airport or seaport on the way out. The minimum is ¥5,000 per store per day, there is no ceiling any more, the category split between general goods and consumables is gone, and so is the special packaging.</p>

<p>The mechanics matter more than the rule. Complete the kiosk check <em>before</em> you check your bag — once the bag is gone, the refund is gone. The check is per receipt: one missing item cancels everything on that receipt. And consumables you have already eaten or used are not eligible.</p>

<p>Korea works the opposite way and still deducts at the till: ₩15,000 minimum per receipt, instant refunds under ₩1,000,000 per receipt and up to ₩5,000,000 per stay, with the original passport scanned before the payment completes. Ask after paying and you are holding a slip you have to claim at the airport instead.</p>

<h2 style="font-size: 1.5em; color: #0f172a; margin-top: 44px; border-bottom: 2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 10px;">4. A 12-day shape that works</h2>

<p>Seoul (3 nights) → Busan (1) → overnight ferry → Fukuoka (1) → Osaka (2) → Kyoto (1) → Tokyo (2) → home. Open-jaw flights in and out of different countries, the crossing in the middle, and no city visited twice. One thing is fixed: the ferry date. Build days 1–4 and 6–12 around it, not the other way round.</p>

<h2 style="font-size: 1.5em; color: #0f172a; margin-top: 44px; border-bottom: 2px solid #e2e8f0; padding-bottom: 10px;">Quick answers</h2>

<p><strong>Can I still take the fast ferry from Busan to Fukuoka?</strong> No. The Queen Beetle has not run since August 2024 and JR Kyushu exited the ferry business in December 2024. Any timetable you find for it is from before that.</p>
<p><strong>Is the Japan Rail Pass worth it for Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka?</strong> No. The 7-day ordinary pass is ¥50,000 on the official site; a Tokyo–Kyoto return on Nozomi is about ¥28,000. It only pays off on a Tokyo–Kyoto–Hiroshima–Hakata–Tokyo loop within seven days.</p>
<p><strong>Do I need a Japanese IC card if I already have T-money?</strong> Yes. They are separate systems with no interchange.</p>
<p><strong>Where do I get the Japanese tax refund now?</strong> At the customs kiosk at the airport, before you check your bag, within 90 days of purchase — or through Visit Japan Web at seven airports including Narita, Haneda, Kansai and Fukuoka.</p>
