Is a K-ETA needed for a 2027 trip?

On 23 November 2026 the Ministry of Justice extended the exemption to 31 December 2027, so an arrival up to that date does not need a K-ETA. The extension covers the countries and regions that are exempt today, Japan included, and the exemption pop-up still appears when you scan your passport on k-eta.go.kr. No decision has been published for 2028 — that page is the final word.

Landed 31 December 2027, leaving 1 January 2028 — which rule applies?

The date that matters is normally the date of entry, and the exemption is written to end at 23:59 Korea time on 31 December 2027. A flight that departs before midnight and lands after it is the grey case: applying for the K-ETA in advance costs ₩10,300 and removes the question.

Is the e-Arrival Card the same as the K-ETA?

No. The K-ETA is an entry approval decided before you fly; the e-Arrival Card is the arrival form you hand in when you land, and it can be submitted online or on paper. The online version is free, opens no earlier than 3 days before arrival, and is not needed by travellers holding a valid K-ETA or by registered residents.
