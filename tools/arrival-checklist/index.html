Why does the K-ETA answer change on 1 January 2027?

The exemption for the 22 countries and regions runs from 1 January to 31 December 2026, Korea time. Past extensions were announced on 11 December 2024 and 23 December 2025, so a 2027 decision is expected late in December 2026. Until it is posted, an arrival on or after 1 January 2027 should assume a K-ETA is needed.

Landed 31 December, leaving 1 January — which rule applies?

The date that matters is normally the date of entry, and the exemption is written to end at 23:59 Korea time on 31 December 2026. A flight that departs before midnight and lands after it is the grey case: applying for the K-ETA in advance costs ₩10,300 and removes the question.

Is the e-Arrival Card the same as the K-ETA?

No. The K-ETA is an entry approval decided before you fly; the e-Arrival Card is the customs-and-immigration form that replaced the paper card on 1 January 2026. It is free, filed online no earlier than 3 days before arrival, and it is not needed by travellers holding a valid K-ETA or by registered residents.
